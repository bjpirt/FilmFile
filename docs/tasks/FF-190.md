# FF-190 A frame's page in a site: the filmstrip above, and its details

**Status:** done · **Group:** 13. Sites · **Built in:** 13. Sites

## Why

Seeing where you are in an event before the photo, and finding out more about a photo (camera, lens, film) only when you want to.

## What

A frame's page gets the new look, with the filmstrip moved above the photo, just under the bar with "← Iceland" and "7 / 42". Under the photo, one line gives its title and date, and a small ⓘ icon shows and hides a short list of the other details chosen for the site, hidden at first. _(From the [wireframes](https://claude.ai/artifact/VWNcdhqokFEmgHq6My8bqn): "Frame · filmstrip above" and "Phone · Frame". Changes FF-184's layout and caption.)_

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] The bar has "← Iceland" (or "← Other 2026 photos") at the left and "7 / 42" at the right, replacing "7 of 42".
- [x] The filmstrip ("Filmstrip") is directly under the bar, centred, above the photo, the current frame outlined; it scrolls sideways on its own when it's wider than the screen. The script scrolls it so the frame shown is in its middle (as near as the strip's ends allow); its thumbnails carry their sizes, so that works before they load. _(Fixed after review: the old centring measured from the wrong box once the strip lost `position: relative`.)_
- [x] The photo is large, between thin "Previous frame" and "Next frame" arrows (none past the ends), fitted so the photo and its caption line fit on the screen.
- [x] Under the photo, one line: the frame's title in bold and its date shot in grey ("Black beach · Sep 2026"), each when ticked in "Details to show"; either is left out when unknown or not ticked, and the line is gone with neither.
- [x] A small ⓘ icon ("Details") at the end of that line shows a list under it of the other details ticked for the site, each a small grey label over its value (Camera, Lens, Film stock), and hides it again. It starts hidden; once shown it stays shown on the next frame, and scrolls into view if it opened below the fold. The icon isn't there when none of the ticked details is known. A few lines of the site's script do it; with scripts off, a stylesheet loaded only then (`<noscript>`, `assets/no-script.css`) shows the list and hides the icon. _(Changed when built, at the user's request: an icon and a little script, not a `<details>` "Details" button.)_
- [x] On the site's page in the app, the captions choice becomes "Details to show", with the same choices; the field stays `captions` in the site file and the API, so nothing changes for saved sites.
- [x] ← and → step, Esc goes back, and a swipe steps on a phone, as now (FF-184).
- [x] On a phone the photo is full width, the filmstrip one row under the bar, and the details two to a row.

## What was built

- Domain: `renderFramePage` writes the bar ("← Frame walk", "3 / 6"), the filmstrip, the stage, then `.frame-info`: the caption line (`<strong>` title, `<span>` date) and, when any is known, the `.info-toggle` icon button with its `#frame-details` list, `hidden`. `site-script.ts` centres the frame shown in the filmstrip (measured on screen, with sized thumbnails), shows and hides the details, keeps it shown from frame to frame (`sessionStorage`, guarded) and scrolls it into view; `no-script-stylesheet.ts` is `assets/no-script.css`, linked from the frame page's `<noscript>`. `captionOrder` is gone. The design version is 4, so sites built before go out of date once.
- Stylesheet: the filmstrip under the bar (slimmer, centred when it fits, a thin line under it), the caption line and the icon, the details grid (two to a row on a phone).
- Web: the fieldset is "Details to show".
- Tests: the frame page (bar and place, filmstrip before the photo, the caption line as chosen and with neither, the details behind the icon, none known, the no-script link and stylesheet), the build, the web label and `build-site.e2e.ts` (the camera hidden, shown from the icon, still shown on the next frame).

## Not in this task

- New details (place, a description of the frame): only those the site can already show.
- Zoom, a slideshow or a download of the full size.
