# FF-118 What an export says about itself

**Status:** to do · **Group:** 12. Exports · **Was:** U96

## Why

An export opened anywhere (Photos, Lightroom, a website) says who took it, when, on what camera and film, and where it's from, unless I choose to keep it plain.

## What

The export dialog (FF-165) gets a "Metadata" choice: "Everything known about the frame" or "Only author and copyright". The files made carry that, written into the JPEG by sharp as it's made (D94). Raws are never written to. Saved exports (FF-117) and the zips of selected frames (FF-167) have the same choice.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The dialog "Export frame 12A" has "Metadata": "Everything known about the frame" (the default) or "Only author and copyright", remembered with the other last-used choices.
- [ ] With everything, a file's EXIF and XMP give its author and copyright (the archive's or the roll's), title, notes as its description, and date shot (as much of it as is known).
- [ ] They give the camera's make and model, the lens and the film stock, with the film stock and the event as keywords too.
- [ ] They give its rating, and its roll and frame number (`2026-014 / 12A`).
- [ ] With only author and copyright, nothing else is written: no camera, lens, film, event, dates, rating, roll or frame number.
- [ ] macOS Preview's inspector and Photos show the author, date, camera and keywords of a file made with everything.

## Not in this task

- Location and GPS (with FF-133, as an option to leave out): none is ever written until then.
- Other formats' metadata, and IPTC fields beyond what sharp writes.

## Notes

- sharp writes EXIF and an XMP packet into images it makes, so no exiftool.
- Building a site (FF-120) uses the same rendering with only author and copyright.
- A saved export's or a build's metadata choice is part of what its files are made from (FF-117), so changing it makes them out of date.
- Tests read the metadata back from the files made.
