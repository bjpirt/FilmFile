# FF-202 Find a roll by its ID

**Status:** to do · **Group:** 16. Internal improvements

## Why

Opening a roll shouldn't get slower as the library grows. Today every lookup of one roll reads and checks every `roll.json` in the library, one lookup at a time, and nearly every request looks one up: each thumbnail, each original, each edit, each frame of an export. On a library of 300 rolls of 36 frames a lookup took about 215 ms, so a roll page's 36 thumbnails spent about 7.5 s on lookups alone.

## What

A roll is found by the ID at the end of its folder's name (`2026-014-Q2P7RM4K/`) and nowhere else: the roll repository, the blob store and the holding area all look only in a folder named with the last 8 characters of the roll's ID, and read only that roll's `roll.json`, which must say the same ID. There's no longer a search through every other folder when that finds nothing (D112, replacing part of D66).

So a roll in a folder not named with its ID (made before D66, or renamed by hand) can no longer be opened. It's shown on the rolls page with the rolls that can't be read, saying what to rename its folder to. FilmFile doesn't rename it itself. _(Recommendation R1 of the architecture review.)_

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A roll in a folder named `<anything>-<last 8 characters of its ID>` is got, saved, changed, renumbered and deleted as now, and its files stored, read and moved to `deleted/` as now.
- [ ] A roll whose folder still has its old number's label after a rename cut short is still found (the ID is at the end of the name), and the next save finishes the rename, as now.
- [ ] A roll in a folder not named with its ID isn't found by its ID: `GET /api/rolls/{rollId}` and every route under it answer 404, and nothing is stored in or moved out of its folder.
- [ ] That roll isn't among the rolls in `GET /api/rolls` or `GET /api/rolls/numbering`. It's listed under `unreadable`, by its folder's name, with the problem "its folder isn't named with its ID; rename it to 2026-014-Q2P7RM4K" (its number's safe label and the last 8 characters of its ID). The rolls page shows it with the rolls that can't be read, with no change to the page.
- [ ] Its number is taken only as any unreadable roll's is, by its folder's name: a folder still called by its number keeps the number; one renamed to something else doesn't.
- [ ] Renaming the folder by hand to the name given makes the roll a normal roll again, with nothing else to do.
- [ ] A roll whose folder is named with its ID but whose `roll.json` can't be read is, as now, not found by its ID, listed under `unreadable` with what's wrong with the file, and its number stays taken.
- [ ] A folder named with a roll's ID whose `roll.json` says another ID isn't taken for that roll's.
- [ ] Another roll's `roll.json` being unreadable or misnamed doesn't stop a roll being got, saved or changed.
- [ ] After a roll is deleted it's no longer found, and deleting it still fails loudly if its folder wasn't taken away.

## Not in this task

- Renaming misnamed folders automatically, on starting or otherwise: it's done by hand, from what the rolls page says.
- Keeping a misnamed roll's number taken from its `roll.json`: a new roll can take the number of a roll whose folder was renamed to something unlike it, until the folder is put right.
- A test that counts the files read. The speed is shown by the measurement in Notes, not guarded by a test.
- A different answer than 404 for a roll whose `roll.json` can't be read.
- Listing rolls, searching frames and building sites, which still read every roll (R6 of the review, and the index, FF-122).
- The repository's three locks, its renumbering rules and its size (R5 of the review).
- Letting browsers cache thumbnails (R3 of the review).

## Notes

- `findRollFolder` in `apps/backend/src/adapters/outbound/filesystem/roll-folder.ts` loses its second pass over `rolls/*/*/roll.json`; it keeps checking that the `roll.json` in a folder named `*-<last 8>` says the ID asked for. Its comment and the file's header comment say the new rule.
- `FilesystemRollRepository.#find` in `filesystem-roll-repository.ts` calls `findRollFolder` in place of `#readAll`, then reads and parses that folder's `roll.json`. `#readAll` stays for `listVersioned`, `#numberTaken` and `saveRenumberedBatch`, and puts a roll that parses but whose folder's name doesn't end with `-<last 8 of its ID>` in `unreadable`, with the name from `rollFolderName`.
- A misnamed roll is an `unreadable` entry like any other (its folder's name and the problem), so its number is reserved as theirs are, by `namedFor` on the folder's name, with no new code.
- Lookups stay inside the repository's archive-wide lock (D77).
- No change to the port, the API's shapes or the files on disk. The in-memory repository is unaffected.
- Tests that pinned the old rule are removed, with the helper that makes a pre-D66 folder: in `apps/backend/test/adapters/filesystem/filesystem-roll-repository.test.ts`, "finds a roll in a folder named by its number alone, as made before D66", "finds a roll whatever its folder is called…" and "renames a folder named by number alone the first time its number changes"; in `filesystem-blob-store.test.ts`, "stores a file in a roll's folder named by number alone, as made before D66". One new adapter test covers the new rule: a roll in a misnamed folder isn't found and is listed under `unreadable` with its problem. The contract suites pass unchanged.
- Measured with a throwaway benchmark on a synthetic library of 300 rolls of 36 frames: one `get` about 215 ms; 36 calls to `findRollFolder`, about 90 ms.
- D112, replacing D66's "found by their `roll.json`, never by the folder's name"; `docs/design.md` section 4 says the same.
